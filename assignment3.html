Five-Class Meme Sentiment Classification Using Images and Text

We propose predicting a meme’s sentiment from its image and associated corrected text. We will compare image-only and text-only classifiers with simple and learned fusion to investigate when the two inputs help each other and when they conflict.

Current stage: preliminary data inspection and proposal preparation. No model performance or instructor approval is claimed. Main implementation and experiments will begin after the dataset proposal is Approved or Approved with conditions.

Task, modalities, input, and output

Track: single-label image–text classification. Each prediction unit is one meme. Its image and supplied text are linked through the same image_name, rather than randomly paired from independent datasets.

Meme image + corrected meme text → two encoders → fusion → five sentiment scores

The target classes are very negative, negative, neutral, positive, and very positive. We retain the original five sentiment annotations rather than merging them into the competition’s three-class Task A. The target label is never supplied as an input. The downloaded CSV does not include the humour, sarcasm, offense, or motivation targets for Tasks B/C.

Corrected text is the wording in the meme, not a separate description of the picture. The full image also contains those printed words, so image-only refers to the input format and does not establish text-free visual reasoning. Primary evaluation assumes corrected text is supplied at prediction time; an image-upload-only demo would require a separately evaluated OCR stage.

Number of paired samples and annotation types

The download contains 6,992 image-linked annotation rows across five sentiment classes. All referenced images exist. Of these, 6,986 pairs are provisionally usable after excluding one image that fails full decoding and five rows with blank corrected text. This count precedes duplicate decisions and semantic pair review.

How we will split: finish the exclusion audit, review duplicate/source/template groups, then assign whole groups using seed 42 while aiming for the target proportions and five-class coverage. Every required model will use the same saved split. We will not randomly split individual rows before grouping.

  • Primary cohort: retain all five classes and all eligible pairs after documented cleaning; no class-balanced subsampling. Preserve original files and record exclusions.
  • Proposed split: approximately 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% test, using seed 42 and class-aware group assignment. Exact sizes will be reported after cleaning and grouping.
  • Split unit: confirmed duplicate/source/template group. Keep all retained records of the same group in one partition, including related memes when identifiable. Review perceptual matches and repeated text as candidates rather than automatically merging them.
  • Duplicate policy: review corrected text before treating identical images as identical pairs. For equivalent pairs with consistent labels, retain one representative; exclude conflicting duplicate pairs under a documented policy rather than inventing labels. Record unresolved cases.
  • Leakage checks: freeze sample/group manifests and hashes before training, check overlap and five-class coverage, and fit vocabulary or learned preprocessing statistics on training data only. State the limits of template detection.
  • Model selection: validation chooses settings and checkpoints; evaluate locked configurations on held-out test data. No test-driven tuning.

Subset rules

No deliberate subsampling is planned. We will retain all five sentiment classes and all valid paired samples after documented cleaning. We will not randomly downsample the dataset or remove majority-class records simply to balance the classes.

  • Validity exclusions: exclude missing or undecodable images, blank corrected text, and records with missing or invalid sentiment labels. Current checks identify one undecodable image and five blank corrected-text rows; no missing images or invalid sentiment labels were found.
  • Equivalent duplicate pairs: after reviewing corrected text, retain one representative when the same underlying image/text pair has consistent labels. Choose the lexicographically smallest image filename among equivalent valid records to make the selection reproducible.
  • Conflicting duplicate pairs: exclude all records of an equivalent pair with conflicting sentiment labels. Do not invent a replacement label. Different or incorrect transcriptions require review before treating records as equivalent.
  • Related but distinct memes: a shared template alone is not an exclusion rule; retain valid distinct pairs and keep confirmed source/template groups in the same split.
  • Audit trail: preserve raw files, save each inclusion/exclusion decision and its reason, and report final total/per-class counts. The current 6,986 eligible pairs are provisional and precede duplicate decisions.

Baseline plan and improved multimodal model

Four planned configurations; none has been trained
ConfigurationPlanned method
Image onlyImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 with frozen encoder and a trainable five-class head
Text onlyTrainable word embeddings, small GRU encoder, and five-class head
Simple fusionProject image/text features, concatenate them, and apply a small MLP
Improved fusionUse a learned sigmoid gate to weight projected image/text features before classification

Both fusion designs combine intermediate features. The proposed gate computes g × image_features + (1 − g) × text_features, with weights learned from both inputs. Each model uses cross-entropy on five raw logits; fusion has one final classification loss and no proposed auxiliary branch losses. Keep image encoder weights and BatchNorm statistics frozen initially.

Preprocessing: RGB images, aspect-preserving resize and padding to 224 × 224, and fixed pretrained normalization. Avoid flips or aggressive crops that alter printed text. Use corrected text, a training-only vocabulary, padding-aware text encoding, and an initial maximum of 64 tokens; measure truncation before fixing that limit. Incomplete paired inputs are outside the primary benchmark.

Metrics, experimental design, and ablation

Primary metric: Macro-F1 across all five classes. Also report accuracy, per-class precision/recall/F1/support, a 5 × 5 confusion matrix, parameter counts, training time, inference cost, and peak GPU memory.

Initial proposed training settings are AdamW, learning rate 0.001 for trainable parameters, weight decay 0.0001, batch size 32, up to 20 epochs, dropout 0.3, and seed 42. Stop after five epochs without validation Macro-F1 improvement; retain the highest validation Macro-F1 checkpoint, choosing the earlier epoch on ties. These settings have not been benchmarked.

Inspect cases where one modality resolves another’s ambiguity and cases where fusion causes errors. Review semantic evidence manually: disagreement among model predictions alone does not prove modality conflict. Fusion is not required to outperform unimodal models; explain outcomes with evidence and avoid statistical claims from a single run.
