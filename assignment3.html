Task, modalities, input, and output

Input / two modalitiesImage + corrected text

224 × 224 RGB meme image and its associated corrected wording, aligned by image filename.

TaskSingle-label classification

Predict the overall sentiment of one meme from genuinely related image and text inputs.

OutputFive sentiment scores

Very negative, negative, neutral, positive, or very positive. The highest score determines the prediction.

Track: single-label image–text classification. Each prediction unit is one meme. Its image and supplied text are linked through the same image_name, rather than randomly paired from independent datasets.

Meme image + corrected meme text → two encoders → fusion → five sentiment scores

The target classes are very negative, negative, neutral, positive, and very positive. We retain the original five sentiment annotations rather than merging them into the competition’s three-class Task A. The target label is never supplied as an input. The downloaded CSV does not include the humour, sarcasm, offense, or motivation targets for Tasks B/C.

Corrected text is the wording in the meme, not a separate description of the picture. The full image also contains those printed words, so image-only refers to the input format and does not establish text-free visual reasoning. Primary evaluation assumes corrected text is supplied at prediction time; an image-upload-only demo would require a separately evaluated OCR stage.

06

Subset rules

No deliberate subsampling is planned. We will retain all five sentiment classes and all valid paired samples after documented cleaning. We will not randomly downsample the dataset or remove majority-class records simply to balance the classes.

  • Validity exclusions: exclude missing or undecodable images, blank corrected text, and records with missing or invalid sentiment labels. Current checks identify one undecodable image and five blank corrected-text rows; no missing images or invalid sentiment labels were found.
  • Equivalent duplicate pairs: after reviewing corrected text, retain one representative when the same underlying image/text pair has consistent labels. Choose the lexicographically smallest image filename among equivalent valid records to make the selection reproducible.
  • Conflicting duplicate pairs: exclude all records of an equivalent pair with conflicting sentiment labels. Do not invent a replacement label. Different or incorrect transcriptions require review before treating records as equivalent.
  • Related but distinct memes: a shared template alone is not an exclusion rule; retain valid distinct pairs and keep confirmed source/template groups in the same split.
  • Audit trail: preserve raw files, save each inclusion/exclusion decision and its reason, and report final total/per-class counts. The current 6,986 eligible pairs are provisional and precede duplicate decisions.

Baseline plan and improved multimodal model

Four planned configurations; none has been trained
ConfigurationPlanned method
Image onlyImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 with frozen encoder and a trainable five-class head
Text onlyTrainable word embeddings, small GRU encoder, and five-class head
Simple fusionProject image/text features, concatenate them, and apply a small MLP
Improved fusionUse a learned sigmoid gate to weight projected image/text features before classification

Both fusion designs combine intermediate features. The proposed gate computes g × image_features + (1 − g) × text_features, with weights learned from both inputs. Each model uses cross-entropy on five raw logits; fusion has one final classification loss and no proposed auxiliary branch losses. Keep image encoder weights and BatchNorm statistics frozen initially.

Preprocessing: RGB images, aspect-preserving resize and padding to 224 × 224, and fixed pretrained normalization. Avoid flips or aggressive crops that alter printed text. Use corrected text, a training-only vocabulary, padding-aware text encoding, and an initial maximum of 64 tokens; measure truncation before fixing that limit. Incomplete paired inputs are outside the primary benchmark.

Problem Statement

Motivation

Sentiment labels can help people browse meme collections or prioritize examples for human review. A caption can change meaning when read with the expression, setting, or visual reference in the meme.

Task formulation

Each prediction uses one meme: its full RGB image xI and supplied corrected transcription xT. The classifier returns five logits z = fθ(xI, xT). The predicted class is the index of the highest logit, with this fixed order: very negative, negative, neutral, positive, very positive.

Research questions

  1. Does combining the aligned image and text improve test Macro-F1 over both unimodal baselines?
  2. Does an input-dependent feature gate improve on a separately trained fixed average?

Main difficulties

  • Cross-modal context: an image can support a caption, add a visual punchline, or contradict its literal meaning.
  • Subjective intensity: positive and very positive share a polarity. Annotators can differ on how strong a meme sounds.
  • Unequal label support: train contains 2,155 positive examples and 102 very-negative examples. Accuracy therefore reflects majority classes strongly.
  • Template and cultural references: a visual joke can rely on familiarity with a character or scene. Exact-duplicate checks cover only part of template reuse.

How the duplicate review and cleaning work
  1. Invalid records: truncated images, blank text and junk text (#NAME?, HTML dumps) are removed.
  2. Exact duplicates: identical decoded pixels are found with a SHA-256 hash of the RGB pixels.
  3. Near duplicates: among pairs with the same text, 64×64 grayscale thumbnails with mean difference ≤ 5 are treated as re-encoded copies.
  4. Conflicts: copies with different labels are all removed because the right label is unknown; same-label copies keep the smallest file name.
  5. Manual review: 20 repeated captions on different images were inspected; same label keeps one and different labels remove all.
  6. Assign: a stratified, group-aware split is made twice (70 / 30, then the 30% halved) and rejected unless every class is in every split.

Bias and limitations

  • Label noise: 16 of 25 exact-image duplicate groups had conflicting labels. The audit also identified conflicting repeated captions. Annotator agreement on unique memes was outside the measured scope.
  • Subjectivity: sentiment is subjective and neighbouring classes (positive vs very positive) overlap.
  • Imbalance: only 147 very-negative pairs, so its metrics are noisy.
  • Redundancy: the text is also visible in the image, so the two modalities are not independent.
  • Watermarks: site names in the text are not sentiment cues.
  • Templates and license: meme templates are reused, and source-license terms should be checked before redistribution.

Methodology

Pipeline

The loader aligns two inputs and a target by image_name. A batch contains RGB images [B, 3, 224, 224], padded token IDs [B, L], and true lengths [B]. All variants output [B, 5] raw logits. Validation Macro-F1 selects the checkpoint before held-out evaluation.

Encoders and fusion

We use intermediate feature-level fusion. The image and text encoders produce separate representations of the same meme. Each fusion model projects both representations to 256 dimensions, combines them, and outputs five logits. The highest logit determines the predicted sentiment.

Parameter counts and implementation

The image branch retains visible text in the original raster. ResNet-18 uses ImageNet IMAGENET1K_V1 weights. Its trainable classifier sits on top of 11,176,512 frozen parameters, with BatchNorm statistics kept fixed. The embedding and GRU are trained from scratch. A linear layer with a inputs and b outputs has (a + 1)b parameters. The gate therefore adds (512 + 1) × 256 = 131,328 parameters over fixed averaging.

PyTorch supplies Embedding, GRU, packing, linear layers, dropout, cross-entropy and AdamW. Torchvision supplies the ResNet architecture and weights. The team implements paired data loading and collation, encoder wrappers, the three feature operators, the training and evaluation loop, and artifact tracking.

Limitations & Conclusion

Limitations

The benchmark supplies corrected text, uses one seed and a custom split, and contains relatively few negative examples. Some meme templates may occur across splits despite exact-duplicate checks. The encoders represent a whole image and caption as vectors, which limits the interaction of individual words and image regions. More seeds and tests with raw OCR and unseen templates would clarify generalization.

Conclusion

Concat gives the best class-balanced score on this five-class benchmark. Learned gating improves on fixed averaging by 0.0027 Macro-F1, but trails concat by 0.0223. On the measured setup, the simple fusion baseline offers the strongest Macro-F1 among the trained models.

Future work

  • Evaluate raw OCR separately to measure sensitivity to transcription errors.
  • Hold out visually or semantically related templates and repeat training with independent seeds.
  • Review high-confidence shared errors and collect independent annotation judgments for rare and adjacent sentiment labels.
