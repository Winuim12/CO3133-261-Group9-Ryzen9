06

Split Unit Used to Prevent Leakage

The proposed split unit is a group of duplicate or potentially near-duplicate images. All retained images in a group go into the same set: train, validation, or test. An image with no detected matches forms its own group.
  1. Exact copiesOne copy is kept when identical images share a label. Identical images with conflicting labels are excluded pending manual review.
  2. Similar images togetherThe provisional split keeps automatically flagged near-duplicate candidates in the same set. These candidates and source identities are reviewed before the split is finalized.
  3. No overlapThis reduces the chance that the model is evaluated on a copy of a training image. Augmentation and any dataset-derived normalization statistics use training data only.
07

Evaluation Metrics

Primary metricMacro-F1

F1 is computed per food class, then averaged across all 30 classes. Each class has equal weight, including those with fewer images.

Additional metricsAccuracy and per-class scores

Accuracy measures the proportion of correctly classified images. Precision, recall, and F1 are also reported for each food class.

Error analysisConfusion matrix

A 30 × 30 matrix shows the true and predicted food classes, revealing which dishes the model commonly confuses.

The checkpoint with the highest validation Macro-F1 is selected. After model selection, all metrics are reported on the held-out test set.

Problem Statement

Image classification

Assignment 2 carries the pipeline from Assignment 1 to a larger and more specialized problem. The track covers a simple baseline, a pretrained model with a documented fine-tuning protocol, a controlled experiment, quantitative and qualitative evaluation, error analysis, and a compute-cost analysis.

Real-world motivation

Many apps need to know which dish is in a photo, without asking the user to type its name:

  • Food delivery and review apps. These apps tag every uploaded photo with its dish name, so a search for bún bò Huế finds the right photos and restaurants.
  • Nutrition and calorie tracking. The app recognises the dish from a photo of the meal and suggests its calories instead of asking the user to look it up.
  • Search by photo. A picture of a dish is enough to find its recipe or a nearby place that serves it.

General models do not solve this out of the box: ImageNet has no Vietnamese dish classes, many dishes look alike in a bowl, and the same dish varies by region, vendor and plating. Recognising 30 common dishes from a single photo is the first building block for these apps.

Research questions

Experiment A: baseline versus pretrained

Does an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 beat a CNN trained from scratch on about 17,500 training images?

Experiment B: frozen versus full fine-tuning

With everything else fixed, does updating the whole backbone improve validation Macro-F1 by at least 0.01 over a frozen backbone, and is the extra cost worth it?

Main difficulties

  • Fine-grained classes. Several noodle soups share a broth, herbs and bowl shape, and steamed cakes wrapped in banana leaves look alike from outside.
  • Large variation within a class. The same dish changes with region, vendor, portion, plating, camera angle and lighting.
  • Background clutter. Photos often show side dishes, hands, tables or several dishes at once, although each image has one label.
  • Class imbalance. Classes range from 444 to 1,530 images, so accuracy alone would reward the frequent dishes.
  • Web-collected data. Exact and near duplicates, some filed under two classes, had to be removed or grouped before splitting to avoid leakage.
  • Compute budget. Every run must fit a single GPU session, so training time and inference cost are reported alongside accuracy.

Learning objectives

How duplicate groups are built
  1. Candidates: 64-bit dHash with Hamming distance ≤ 8 yields 1,718 candidate pairs.
  2. Confirm: a candidate is confirmed when the 32 × 32 grayscale thumbnail difference is ≤ 30 / 255. Without it, similar plate layouts chained into groups of 250+ images; with it the largest group is 9.
  3. Group: 480 same-label pairs form 412 groups.
  4. Review conflicts: of 54 cross-label pairs, 25 were one picture under two dishes (both removed) and 29 were different dishes (kept).
  5. Assign: within each class, groups are shuffled with seed 42, test is filled to ~15%, then validation to ~15%, and the rest goes to train.

Fine-tuning procedure

  1. Frozen: the backbone is set to requires_grad = False and kept in eval mode, so BatchNorm statistics remain fixed. Full: all layers are trainable, and BatchNorm statistics are updated.
  2. Adam receives only trainable parameters at learning rate 10−4, ten times lower than the scratch CNN, to limit early drift from pretrained weights; a single rate is used without warm-up.
  3. Data, augmentation and epoch budget match the baseline; the epoch with the highest validation Macro-F1 is retained.

Training setup

All runs use Adam with cross-entropy, batch size 64, a 20-epoch budget, a constant learning rate, seed 42 and AMP FP16. Augmentation is the only regularization; weight decay, dropout and label smoothing are not used. Early stopping (patience 5) and checkpoint selection both monitor validation Macro-F1, and no run stopped early. No hyperparameter search was run.

Training behavior

  • Scratch CNN underfits. Training and validation accuracy stay close at about 23%, validation loss fluctuates, and both curves are still rising at epoch 20.
  • Frozen ResNet-50 converges smoothly. The train–validation gap stays small (75.2% versus 72.6% accuracy at epoch 20), and validation Macro-F1 still gains about 0.004 per epoch near the end.
  • Full ResNet-50 overfits after a fast start. Validation Macro-F1 reaches 0.814 after one epoch and plateaus near 0.87 from epoch 3. Training accuracy climbs to 99% while validation loss rises from 0.47 to about 0.57.
  • Implementation fixes. The local M2 runs showed low GPU utilization, so the final runs add AMP, channels-last memory format, persistent workers and resumable checkpoints.

Qualitative results

Confident correct predictions are close-up photos of a single dish. Low-confidence correct predictions are mostly table scenes with several dishes. Confident errors fall between visually similar dishes, such as Bún bò Huế and Phở or Bánh cuốn and Gỏi cuốn, and on mixed platters where another dish dominates the frame.

Comparison & Discussion

Both hypotheses are supported. Pretraining adds 0.53 test Macro-F1 over the scratch CNN, and full fine-tuning adds another 0.16 over the frozen backbone, far above the 0.01 threshold.

Each gain is the Macro-F1 of the second model minus the first.
ExperimentVal gainTest gainCriterionVerdict
A: CNN → Frozen ResNet-50+0.5347+0.5294Higher validation Macro-F1Supported, but not attributable to one factor
B: Frozen → Full ResNet-50+0.1579+0.1608Gain ≥ 0.01Supported; full fine-tuning is preferred

Where the differences appear

  • Full fine-tuning improves all 30 classes. The largest test F1 gains are on noodle soups and lookalike cakes: Bánh đúc +0.39 (0.36 → 0.75), Bún riêu +0.31, Bún mắm +0.30, Hủ tiếu +0.30, Mì Quảng +0.27 and Phở +0.25.
  • Distinctive dishes gain little. Bánh mì, Bánh bèo, Cá kho tộ and Bún đậu mắm tôm already exceed 0.82 F1 when frozen and gain at most 0.05.
  • The scratch CNN collapses toward the largest class. Its most frequent errors all predict Bún bò Huế, and five classes (Bánh căn, Bánh canh, Bánh đúc, Bún mắm, Cao lầu) score 0 F1.

Likely causes

  • Data scale and initialization. About 17,500 images and 20 epochs are not enough for a 21 M-parameter plain CNN to learn food features from random weights. ImageNet features already encode textures, shapes and bowls.
  • Fixed features limit the frozen model. The noodle soups differ mainly in broth colour, noodle shape and toppings. Generic ImageNet features keep them close together, so a linear head cannot separate them well.
  • Backbone adaptation drives the full model. Updating every layer lets the features learn these fine-grained cues. Without weight decay, the model also memorizes the training set (99% training accuracy), and validation Macro-F1 gains only 0.008 after epoch 5.
  • Class imbalance is not the main factor. Accuracy and Macro-F1 stay within 0.01 of each other for both ResNet-50 models, so errors are not concentrated in small classes.

Accuracy, speed and parameter trade-offs

  • Full fine-tuning is cheap for its gain. It trains 383× more parameters than the frozen model but takes only 9% longer (60 versus 55 minutes), because both pass every image through the full backbone. Inference speed is identical; only the checkpoint grows (270 versus 91 MB).
  • The CNN is fastest but not useful. It runs at 1,534 images/s, about 1.9× the ResNet-50 throughput, yet reaches only 0.19 Macro-F1. It also trains the longest (65 minutes), which suggests that data loading rather than model compute dominates epoch time; this has not been profiled.

Conclusions not yet supported

  • All results come from one seed, so run-to-run variance is unknown, and small gaps such as the 0.008 after epoch 5 are not meaningful.
  • Experiment A changes model, initialization and learning rate together, so it cannot show which one causes the gain.
  • A longer CNN schedule, or full fine-tuning with weight decay and earlier stopping, could change the margins; neither was tested.
  • Timings come from a shared Kaggle T4 and are indicative rather than controlled measurements.

By true family, the error rate is highest for noodle soups (16.9%) and steamed cakes (13.2%), and lowest for rice dishes (3.4%). The weakest classes are Bánh canh (F1 0.705), Bánh đúc (0.747), Hủ tiếu (0.770) and Phở (0.777).

Cross-family errors: sample review

  • Multi-dish or cluttered scenes (8 of 30): trays, street stalls and people eating hide the labelled dish, as in Cơm tấm → Bún thịt nướng.
  • Unusual view or cooking stage (8 of 30): close crops, a lidded pot or a cake still in the pan, as in Bánh xèo → Bánh pía.
  • Soups from another family (8 of 30): noodle soups predicted as Canh chua or Cháo lòng, and the reverse.
  • Shared wrapping or components (4 of 30): banana-leaf packages and grilled meat, as in Nem chua → Bánh tét.
  • Doubtful labels (2 of 30): the image does not appear to show the labelled dish.

Confidence of errors

225 errors (49%) have confidence ≥ 0.8, so the model is often confidently wrong; only 53 errors fall below 0.5. The true class is still in the top 3 for 70% of errors. Temperature scaling or label smoothing could make confidence usable as a reject signal, and a top-3 output would recover most mistakes in a search or tagging app.

Limitations & Conclusion

Answer to the research questions: ImageNet pretraining is essential at this data scale, and full fine-tuning is clearly worth its cost. Full ResNet-50 reaches test Macro-F1 0.8787 and accuracy 87.8%.

Limitations

  • Data: web photos favour styled review images, and label noise on unique images remains. Heavily edited copies may still leak across splits despite the duplicate audit.
  • Method: the single-label setup has no way to handle multi-dish photos, which drive a large share of cross-family errors. The full model overfits without weight decay, and its confidence is poorly calibrated.
  • Experiment: the evidence rests on one seed per configuration, and Experiment A does not isolate a single factor. The other open points are listed under Comparison & Discussion.
  • Generalization: the model covers only 30 dishes and forces any other food into one of them. Results may not hold for everyday phone photos or regional variants missing from the data.

Future work

  • Repeat each configuration over 3 or more seeds and report mean ± standard deviation.
  • Add weight decay and stop at the validation plateau around epoch 5 to cut overfitting and training time.
  • Test a higher input resolution for the noodle-soup and griddle-cake confusions.
  • Crop to the main dish, calibrate confidence, and return top-3 predictions for application use.
